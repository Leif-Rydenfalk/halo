halo — open AirTag-compatible tracker

Factory handoff pack · generated 2026-09-05 · commit d07e576

halo is an open-source tracker that behaves like an Apple AirTag: it is found through Apple's Find My network, it makes a sound, a phone can tap it, and it wakes on motion. Every design file is public. This pack states exactly what is finished, what is not, and what we need a factory to measure that a computer cannot.

halo 是一个开源追踪器，功能对标 Apple AirTag：可通过 Apple Find My 网络定位、可发声、支持手机 NFC 触碰读取、运动唤醒。全部设计文件公开。本文件说明：哪些已完成、哪些未完成，以及哪些必须由工厂实测、计算机无法给出答案。

5 · Open risks, stated plainly

  • The submission tool refuses halo today, on three gates, and that is correct — Run against the real package it exits 1 and sends nothing: the artifacts are 6,461 s older than the board, the board has 83 unconnected items, and two manufacturability rules fail. One gate PASSES — identity — because it is a genuine four-layer export of the WRONG REVISION, which is exactly why freshness is a separate gate from identity. Proven on a board that IS ready: ce-pcb's routed example passes all six gates with 28 rules clean and zero unconnected, and the bundle stages.
  • A physical law had been turned into a tolerance — The antenna's sanity check required an implied permittivity above 1, because a dielectric cannot speed a wave up. To admit a failing case, that floor had been widened to 0.64. The verification lane found it, and the number it was admitting came from a run that hit its timestep limit without converging and shows no reactance zero-crossing anywhere in the sweep — meaning there is no resonance at all, and the 2.425 GHz figure was not a resonance. Both the floor and the convergence table have been corrected. This is the exact prohibition the project runs on: a row closed by weakening its check is worse than an open row, because it stops anyone looking.
  • The antenna does not yet resonate in the Bluetooth band — Two round-board geometries return a passing verdict while resonating at 4.0 and 5.8 GHz, because their assertions test only how deep the match is and how efficient the radiator is, never the frequency. Caught by the orchestrator on 2026-09-03, the assertion is being made mandatory and the geometry retuned. This is stated here rather than hidden because a passing test that checks the wrong thing is the most dangerous artifact in any release pack.
  • The sounder is unproven and legally required — See question one. If a bonded bender cannot reach 60 Phon at 25 cm, the fallback is a micro-speaker, which costs more than the rest of the board.
  • Nothing has been built — Every result in this pack comes from a simulator or an emulator. Eight claims are tracked in docs/VERIFICATION-DEBT.md with the measurement that would close each; four of them need hardware.
  • The sounder part is resolved (was: may not be buyable) — Closed 2026-09-04. The Murata part D11a chose is obsolete with zero stock and delisted from LCSC. Replaced by Same Sky CEB-2021 at 0.21 mm, thinner than assumed so the stack is unaffected, 3,756 in stock, with two documented alternates. See DECISIONS.md D20. What remains open is not the part but its loudness, which only a measurement settles.
  • Every gerber export had been failing silently — The factory tooling lane found that a flag it passed to the board exporter does not exist in this version, so no gerber export had ever succeeded, while the pipeline reported success. Same defect class as the router that never ran and the antenna that asserted the wrong quantity — see docs/TOOLS-THAT-LIE.md. Found by measurement, not review.
  • The mandated sound level has no production limit yet — The anti-stalking standard specifies 60 Phon, which is a loudness level under ISO 532-1. A sound level meter does not measure phon; it measures decibels. So the production line's decibel gate has no anchor until a golden unit is measured in phon and correlated, which is a qualification task and a gauge study, not a line test. Until then the loudest requirement in the product is untestable on the line. This is the single sharpest open item in the pack.
  • The flash sits on an unregulated coin-cell rail — The revision A schematic puts the SPI flash on the raw CR2032 rail at 3.0 V, while the part is rated 1.65 to 2.0 V. Found by the test lane reading the schematic, not by a simulation. The board lane has been asked either to regulate it or to delete it — deleting is likely, since the entire firmware is 13,164 bytes and fits in 1.27 percent of the chip's own memory.
  • Stock cover is thin at volume — The main chip covers a thousand units once over and ten thousand not at all; 12 of 22 lines cannot cover ten thousand three times over. This is a question for a factory with purchasing relationships, not a design problem — but it is the reason the cost model's upper quantities should be read as indicative.
  • The spec states no battery-gauge accuracy requirement — Found by the availability lane while checking whether ±1 percent resistors were needed. They are affordable, so it no longer gates anything — but the requirement itself is simply absent from SPEC.md, which is a hole rather than a decision. Recorded as CANNOT DETERMINE.
